03 / TALKS

Titles & abstracts

PRASUNA BANDI · TALK 01

Introduction to Diophantine Approximation

The rational numbers are dense in the real line, but this leaves an important question unanswered: how accurately can we approximate a number using a fraction with a small denominator? Why is 355/113 such a good approximation to π, and are some irrational numbers harder to approximate than others? Starting from the pigeonhole principle, we will prove Dirichlet’s approximation theorem and explore how continued fractions produce remarkably accurate rational approximations. We will then see how questions about rational approximation can be viewed from the perspective of dynamical systems. The lecture will emphasise examples and elementary proofs.

PRASUNA BANDI · TALK 02

An Introduction to Ergodic Theory

Can a completely deterministic rule produce behaviour that resembles randomness? We will investigate this question through two simple systems: rotation of a circle and the map x ↦ 2x (mod 1). These examples lead to questions about recurrence, the distribution of orbits, and the frequencies of digits in number expansions. Along the way, we will see how rational approximation controls the motion of an irrational rotation and how binary digits encode the dynamics of the doubling map.

RENJITH T

A Path from Variational Calculus to Topology

This talk explores how classical optimization problems naturally lead to the concepts of metric and topological spaces. Starting with minimization problems in the calculus of variations, we look at what happens when the unknown is a curve rather than a point in Rn. We examine how comparing such curves requires suitable notions of distance, why completeness is essential, and how the behavior of infinite-dimensional spaces motivates weak topologies. The aim is to provide an intuitive bridge from calculus to abstract topology.

MANUDARSAN B L

When Mathematics Says No

Mathematics is often seen as a discipline where every problem has a solution waiting to be discovered. But is that always the case? From the impossibility of classical geometric constructions and the limitations of algebraic formulas to Hilbert’s tenth problem, Turing’s halting problem, and Gödel’s incompleteness theorem, this talk explores some remarkable boundaries of mathematical knowledge. Through these examples, we will discover how mathematics not only solves problems but also reveals why certain problems cannot be solved by particular methods.
